About

I am Youssef Kabbaj, a master's student in Applied Physics at EPFL. I work on statistical physics and generative machine learning. I want my work in machine learning to have a direct use for people. So far, most of it has been on proteins, RNA and medical imaging.

My approach to a problem is the same each time: understand it first, represent it under a simple model to get a feel for the aspects that matter, then optimise for real data. In most of my projects I start by building an exact reference, such as a synthetic distribution or the optimum of a control problem, so that I can see how far a model is from the right answer.

I have moved between quite different fields, from plasma physics to quantum chemistry, protein statistics and medical imaging. Each time, I had to learn the basics of a new domain quickly and then contribute to it. Before my internship at Bracco, for example, I had never worked with ultrasound.

Outside work, I play basketball and enjoy sport in general, and I like thinking about the larger questions in life. I will bring home-made tiramisu to the office.

  2. Feb to Aug 2026

    Deep learning for contrast-enhanced ultrasound

    Industry internship, Bracco Suisse S.A.

    I built two models for the company's ultrasound sequences: an optical-flow model for motion correction and a localisation model. Real clinical acquisitions have no ground truth, so a model cannot be checked directly. I wrote a synthetic data generator for each model, reproducing the appearance and statistics of real acquisitions, so that both could be measured against known answers.

    On synthetic data, the motion-correction error was about 5%, and the localisation precision and accuracy were both above 95%. For the real data, I designed reference-free metrics, used them to find where the models failed, and fed those failures back into the generators.

    Semester project, Laboratory of Computational Chemistry and Biochemistry, EPFL

    I ran QM/MM simulations of acetone in MiMiC, with GROMACS and CP2K, and measured how the placement of the boundary between the quantum and classical regions changes the computed energies and vibrational frequencies.

  4. Feb to Jul 2025

    Biased generative protein models based on attention DCA

    Summer internship and semester project, Laboratory of Statistical Biophysics, EPFL

    I fitted a Potts model to a protein family, with the couplings parametrised by a factored self-attention layer and trained by pseudo-likelihood. Generated sequences reproduce the natural one-site frequencies at r = 0.99 and the two-site correlations at r = 0.87, against a train-versus-test ceiling of 0.94.

    To steer generation, I wrote conditioning on PCA coordinates exactly as a harmonic restraint on the Potts energy, which needs no retraining. An under-sampled sub-family went from 5% to 100% of the generated chains while keeping its functional motif.

Personal projects

Generative models for protein sequences

Real protein families have no known distribution to compare against, so I built one: a tree of Potts models with 3 families, 9 subfamilies and 18 clades, whose parameters are known exactly. I sampled 180,000 sequences from it and trained generative models on them.

A factored-attention Potts model matched the pooled statistics but got the proportions wrong when it had no labels. A masked diffusion model, trained without labels, recovered the family shares and reached a pair-correlation r of 0.992, against 0.994 for true samples. Generation decides the family after about 2 revealed residues out of 60, the subfamily after about 5 and the clade after about 8.

Code on GitHub

RNA reactivity prediction (Kaggle Ribonanza)

A transformer with 3.6 million parameters that predicts the chemical reactivity of each nucleotide from sequence, trained on about 750,000 noisy sequencing measurements. The usual practice is to discard the low-quality data. I kept it as an auxiliary target and added a head that predicts base-pairing probabilities. Together, these reduced the held-out error by 11% at equal compute.

Reinforcement learning for microswimmer navigation

A microswimmer has to reach a target through a noisy flow at low Reynolds number. I trained a policy with reinforcement learning and compared it with the exact dynamic-programming optimum for each task. On the task set shown here, the policy always arrives, in a median of 1.08 times the optimal time.

Drift-field inference from stochastic trajectories

Recovering the drift field of an overdamped stochastic process from simulated trajectories. I compared five estimators, from binned Kramers–Moyal to an amortised CNN that infers the whole field in one forward pass.

Code on GitHub

Fluctuation theorems on a pulled polymer

A GPU Langevin simulation of a bead-spring chain pulled through a thermal bath by a moving trap, with 10⁵ trajectories per protocol. I tested the Jarzynski equality and the Crooks theorem against an exact free-energy reference. The one-sided Jarzynski estimate has a bias of 1.5 kT for a 40-bead chain, and Bennett's acceptance ratio recovers the free-energy difference within its ±0.04 kT error bar.
